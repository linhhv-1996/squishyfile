## When MOV files stop working outside Apple devices

MOV is Apple's native video format, used by iPhone, iPad, Mac, and QuickTime. Inside the Apple ecosystem it works well — but the moment you send a MOV file to someone on Windows or Android, or try to upload it to a platform that expects MP4, compatibility problems start to appear. The file may not play, the upload may fail, or the editor may throw a codec error.

Converting MOV to MP4 solves this without re-encoding at a lower quality. MP4 is supported almost everywhere: Windows, Android, most browsers, social media platforms, cloud drives, and the vast majority of video editors. Squishyfile converts MOV to MP4 in your browser using WebAssembly and FFmpeg. The video is processed on your device — nothing is uploaded to a server, no account needed, no watermark.

## When it makes sense to convert

Not every MOV file needs to be converted. If you are only watching the video in QuickTime or the Photos app on your own Mac, MOV works fine as-is. But there are plenty of situations where MP4 is the better format:

- You want to send an iPhone video to someone on Android or Windows and know it will open without issues
- You are uploading a video to Instagram, YouTube, Google Drive, or another platform that handles MP4 more reliably
- You are attaching a video to an email or work document and do not want the recipient to deal with compatibility problems
- Your video editing software imports MOV with errors or instability
- You want to keep a long-term archive copy in a format that will still be playable on any device years from now

MOV files from iPhones and Macs can also vary in their internal codec. Depending on your recording settings, the video may use H.264 or HEVC — and HEVC MOV files in particular can cause playback issues on older Windows machines and some Android devices. MP4 with H.264 is the most universally supported combination.

**Step 1 — Choose a MOV file.** Drag your MOV video into the upload area or click to select a file from your device. The tool works with MOV files saved locally on your computer, iPhone, or tablet. It does not download videos from URLs.

## iPhone MOV files and why they sometimes cause problems

iPhones record in a few different formats depending on your settings. Older recording settings and some export scenarios produce MOV files — especially when you are using QuickTime, AirDrop to a Mac, or exporting from the Photos app.

The internal codec matters. iPhones that record in HEVC produce MOV files that are efficient in storage but can be difficult to open on Windows without additional codec packs. If you have ever shared an iPhone video and the other person could not open it, the HEVC MOV format is often the reason. Converting to MP4 — typically with H.264 encoding — removes that barrier.

## Converting on Mac, Windows, iPhone, and Android

Because Squishyfile runs in your browser, it works the same way on macOS, Windows, iPhone, and Android. On a Mac, drag the MOV from Finder into the upload area. On Windows, use File Explorer. On iPhone, open the page in Safari and choose a video from the Files app or Photos.

MOV files from iPhone cameras can be very large, especially 4K videos and long recordings. For these, a desktop or laptop browser will be more stable and faster than a mobile browser. If conversion stalls on mobile, switch to a computer. Keep the screen active and do not close the tab while the file is being processed.
