Use Squishyfile to compress video online in your browser. Select an MP4, MOV, WebM, MKV, AVI, WMV, or FLV file, choose a compression mode, and download a smaller MP4 that is easier to send, upload, or store.

The compressor runs locally with WebAssembly and FFmpeg. Your video does not need to be uploaded to a server for compression, so it is useful for personal clips, work recordings, school videos, client previews, and other files you prefer to keep on your device.

## Quick steps

1. Choose a video from your device or drag it into the upload area.
2. Pick **Balanced**, **Smallest size**, **High quality**, or set a **Target file size**.
3. Click **Compress Now** and keep the tab open while the browser processes the file.
4. Download the compressed MP4 and quickly check the result before deleting the original.

For most videos, start with **Balanced**. Use **Smallest size** when the upload limit is strict. Use **High quality** for videos with text, faces, product details, slides, or screen recordings. Use **Target file size** when the final video needs to stay under a specific limit such as 8MB, 15MB, 25MB, 50MB, or 100MB.

## Files you can use

Squishyfile supports common formats including MP4, MOV, WebM, MKV, AVI, WMV, and FLV. The compressed result is saved as MP4 because it works well across phones, computers, browsers, email clients, chat apps, and social platforms.

This tool can also help with iPhone MOV videos. Choose the MOV file, compress it in the browser, and download a smaller MP4 that is easier to share on Windows, Android, email, messaging apps, and social media.

## Good fit for

Use this page when a video is too large for email, WhatsApp, Discord, Messenger, Telegram, Instagram, cloud upload, or phone storage. It is also useful before sending large recordings from an iPhone, Android phone, camera, meeting app, screen recorder, or video editor.

Compression results depend on video length, resolution, frame rate, bitrate, motion, and noise. Screen recordings, slides, webcam clips, tutorials, and fixed-camera videos usually shrink well. Fast motion, 4K, dark scenes, gaming footage, and shaky video may need a larger output size to stay clear.
