## Convert text into Japanese 400-character manuscript pages

Use this manuscript counter to estimate how many pages your text would fill on Japanese manuscript paper, known as **原稿用紙**. Paste your draft and the tool converts the text length into 400-character pages, the standard 20 × 20 grid often used for Japanese essays, reports, school assignments, novels, contest entries, and formal submissions.

This page is focused on **manuscript page calculation**, not general character counting or word counting. If you only need the total number of characters, use the [Character Counter](/character-counter). If your requirement is based on words, use the [Word Counter](/word-counter). If you are checking whether a post fits X, Instagram, YouTube, or another social platform, use the [SNS Character Limit Checker](/sns-character-limit).

## What a 400-character manuscript page means

Japanese manuscript paper is usually arranged as 20 columns by 20 rows, giving **400 character cells per page**. A manuscript counter turns a normal text draft into an estimated number of manuscript pages, so you can understand whether your writing is close to 1 page, 3 pages, 5 pages, 10 pages, or another required length.

This is useful when a guideline says something like **400字詰め原稿用紙で3枚**, **原稿用紙5枚程度**, or **400-character manuscript pages**. Instead of manually dividing the character count by 400 each time you edit, you can paste the text and see the page estimate immediately.

The result is best understood as a practical estimate. Real manuscript formatting may depend on the rules of a school, publisher, contest, or client. Some instructions count punctuation, spaces, line breaks, numbers, and half-width characters in a specific way, so official submission rules should always come first.

## When to use a manuscript counter

A manuscript counter is most useful when the required length is expressed in Japanese manuscript paper pages rather than raw characters or words. This often appears in Japanese writing assignments, book reports, essays, short stories, literary submissions, and language-learning tasks.

Students can use it to check whether an essay reaches the required number of pages before submission. Writers can estimate the volume of short stories, chapters, columns, and contest drafts. Editors and teachers can compare revisions by manuscript-page volume instead of looking only at a raw character count.

For example, if a task asks for around 3 manuscript pages, the target is roughly 1,200 characters before considering special formatting rules. If the requirement is 5 pages, the rough target is around 2,000 characters. The counter helps you stay close to that range while drafting and editing.

## Manuscript pages vs character count vs word count

Manuscript page count, character count, and word count are related, but they are not the same metric. A manuscript page estimate answers the question: **how many 400-character Japanese manuscript pages would this text occupy?** A character counter answers: **how many characters are in this text?** A word counter answers: **how many words are in this draft?**

For Japanese writing, manuscript pages and character count are often more useful than word count. For English writing, word count is usually the clearer metric. For SEO titles, meta descriptions, product fields, database limits, and short forms, character count is usually the better fit.

Keeping these tools separate helps avoid mixed intent. Use this page when the requirement mentions 原稿用紙, 400字詰め, manuscript sheets, or Japanese 400-character pages. Use the character counter when the limit is a strict number of characters. Use the word counter when the instruction is based on words.

## How to use the result while editing

The page estimate is most useful when you treat it as an editing guide. If your draft is below the target, add examples, explanations, transitions, or evidence that make the text more complete. If your draft is above the target, remove repeated points, shorten long sentences, and combine paragraphs that say the same thing.

Because the counter updates as the text changes, you can revise naturally and watch the manuscript page count move toward the required range. This is easier than repeatedly calculating characters by hand or copying the text into a spreadsheet.

The additional metrics also help you understand the structure of the draft. Characters show the raw length. Characters without spaces help when whitespace is not counted. Lines and paragraphs show whether the text is broken up clearly. Reading time gives a quick sense of how long the piece may feel to a reader.

## Useful for Japanese, mixed-language, and translated drafts

This tool is designed around Japanese manuscript-paper calculation, but it can still be useful for mixed Japanese-English drafts, translated texts, and multilingual writing. If a Japanese course, publisher, or contest asks for a 400-character manuscript-page estimate, the tool gives you a practical starting point even when the text contains Latin letters, numbers, punctuation, or mixed scripts.

For official submissions, check the exact counting rules. Some organizations may treat full-width and half-width characters differently. Some may count punctuation as one cell. Some may have special rules for line breaks, indentation, ruby text, or vertical writing. The counter is useful for quick planning, but the final check should follow the rule sheet.
